Three concierge sites, built out

Same architecture, three visual systems. The positioning moves off document counts and onto what you actually sell: a private concierge who runs the hardest licence in America so the client barely touches it.

The section architecture — identical in all three

01Hero + live eligibilityThe promise, one primary action, and the two-minute check running inline rather than behind a click. Stripe's trick: put the product in the first viewport.
02Yours / OursNEWThe concierge proposition made literal — a two-column split where your column is nearly empty and ours overflows. This is the single most persuasive block on the page.
03Your case, liveThe case file: one next move, everything else handled. Your strongest existing asset, moved from screen four to screen three.
04Your conciergeNEWA named human with a photo, a direct line and a response time. This is the difference between a service and a concierge, and it is the thing your site is most conspicuously missing.
05Timeline & priceHonest, unglamorous, specific. A concierge that tells you the six-month truth up front is worth more than one that doesn't.
06Counsel on callNEWThe Brecher partnership as a feature, not a footnote — when a case needs a lawyer, one is already attached.
07ClosingOne action. The whole page has been building to it; don't dilute it with a link farm.

Notes before you pick

The concierge section is the one to argue about. A named person with a face, a response time and a direct line is what separates a concierge from a service — and it is the single thing your site is most conspicuously missing. It also commits you operationally: if the page promises a four-hour reply, that has to be true. The initials in the mock are placeholders; this needs a real person and a real photo.

Yours / Ours does the selling. Three items against eleven, side by side, is a more persuasive argument than any paragraph on the current site. It is also the most mobile-friendly block here — it stacks into two short cards.

The eligibility check moves into the hero. Running live in the first viewport rather than sitting behind a button is the Stripe move; it turns the headline into something you can touch. It is also your best lead capture, so putting it first is a conversion decision as much as a design one.

Everything here is a static composition. No ambient animation, no blur pools painting every frame. Motion belongs on scroll and hover, which is what keeps this fast on the phone where the current site falls over.
