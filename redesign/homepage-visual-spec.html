Official-source guide

NYC concealed-carry requirements.

A plain-language map of the documents, training, references, disclosures, and applicant responsibilities that shape a New York City carry application.

Reviewed September 20268 minute readPrimary sources linked
On this page License track References Disclosures Questions

The application is not one form. It is a coordinated file whose requirements change with license type, household, history, and timing.

Start with the correct license track.

New York City uses different application paths for concealed carry, premises residence, and renewal. The right starting point determines the training, reference, and document requirements that belong in your checklist.

You remain the applicant.

We organize and prepare your file. You review the completed materials, control your NYPD account, and submit your own application.

References and household statements.

The standard NYC carry path calls for four character references. Adults who live with you may also need to complete household statements. Each request should be tracked from outreach through signature and notarization where required.

Build the complete record.

Disclosure intake should surface the full history, including sealed or dismissed arrests. Records can then be requested and organized against the published requirement. A question requiring judgment about a specific matter belongs with a New York-licensed attorney.

Source trail · NYPD License Division application instructions and current New York State firearms training requirements. Verify the current official instructions before filing.

Common questions.

Do sealed or dismissed arrests still need to be disclosed?

Yes. The disclosure process includes sealed and dismissed matters. Questions about how a particular history should be addressed require legal counsel.

Who submits the application?

You do. The applicant reviews and submits through their own NYPD account; Gun License NYC does not access applicant credentials or file on anyone’s behalf.

How long should I plan for?

About six months is a useful planning horizon, but actual timing belongs to the NYPD and varies by case. No private service can control the agency’s timing or decision.
