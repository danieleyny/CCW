Gun License NYC · Marketing site

Three directions for the redesign

Mobile first, because that is where it fails hardest. Each direction is a complete visual system — ground, accent, type and the shape of the first screen — not a skin on what exists.

What is actually wrong

The brass is the problem

Gold on near-black is the luxury default — steakhouse, whisky, watch ad, crypto. It cannot read technological. And it is fighting the cyan: two accents, one warm-old, one cool-tech, pulling opposite directions on every screen.

Every section has the same weight

Eleven full-width blocks, each with a bracketed eyebrow, a headline, a paragraph and a visual, in the same rhythm. Nothing dominates, so nothing lands. That sameness is the "generic template" feeling.

It was composed for a desktop

Text-left / visual-right splits collapse into a long text scroll on a phone. The film strip and the document grid are wide-format ideas with no mobile equivalent.

Three illustration languages

A flat vector person at a desk, a paper-document grid, and a product UI mock — three styles on one page. The vector person is the weakest thing on the site; the product mock is the strongest.

The copy is not the problem

"24 documents. 13 stages. One thing on your plate." is a better headline than the one you're running. The honest pricing table and the refile promise are real assets. Keep the words; change everything around them.

One more thing worth saying

Stripe and nas.io — the two sites you named — are both predominantly light. Your site is near-black. I don't think that's a coincidence, so two of these three directions are light.

Direction 01

Aperture

DARK · SPECTRAL GRADIENT
REGISTER: LINEAR / VERCEL

Keep the dark, throw out the gold. One cool spectral accent — violet through blue to cyan — used as light rather than as a colour: a single bloom behind the product, a gradient only on the words that matter. Everything else is white type on near-black with a lot of air.

Ledger

LIGHT · EDITORIAL SERIF

What is true in all three

The product is the hero image. The case file — one next move, seventeen of twenty-four handled — is the strongest thing you own, and it is currently buried three screens down. In every direction it sits in the first viewport, on mobile too. The flat vector illustration goes.

Eleven sections become five: the hero with the case file, the twenty-four documents, the five phases, the honest price, and one closing call. Everything else moves to its own page — the content is good, the homepage is just not where all of it belongs.

No always-on animation above the fold. Every visual here is a static composition. Motion earns its place on scroll or on hover, never as ambient background, which is what is currently costing you the first three seconds.
