The short version: a **premises license** lets you possess a handgun at one specified place — your home or your business — while a **carry license** lets you carry a handgun concealed on your person in public. New York issues these as distinct license types under [NY Penal Law §400.00(2)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/400.00), and choosing the right one starts with being honest about what you actually need the handgun *for*. This guide walks through the difference, who each fits, and how to decide.

## What each license actually authorizes

Under [NY Penal Law §400.00(2)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/400.00), New York issues distinct handgun license types. A **premises license** authorizes possessing a handgun at a specified location — a dwelling or a place of business. An **unrestricted carry license** authorizes carrying a handgun concealed, without regard to a particular place of possession.

The distinction that trips people up is what a premises license does *not* cover. As [NY Penal Law §400.00](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/400.00) sets out, a premises license does not authorize carrying a handgun in public. It covers possession at the licensed location, with only limited lawful transport — for example, directly to and from an authorized range. It is emphatically not a "carry it discreetly" license. If you're picturing having the handgun on you as you move through the city, that's the carry license, not premises.

Our dedicated [premises vs. carry breakdown](/premises-vs-carry) lays the two side by side in more detail; this guide is about how to *choose*.

## Who a premises license fits

A premises license fits you if your honest need is possession in one place. The classic cases:

- **Home defense.** You want a handgun in your residence and have no need to carry it in public. A premises-residence license is built for exactly this.
- **A place of business.** You own or run a business and want a handgun on the premises. A premises-business license covers possession at that location.
- **Range shooting.** You want to own a handgun to shoot at a range, transporting it there and back within what the law allows.

If that's your real situation, the premises license is the honest, lower-friction fit — you're not asking for authority you don't intend to use, and the application reflects the narrower need.

## Who a carry license fits

The carry license is for the person whose genuine need is to have the handgun *on them* in public. New York recognizes an unrestricted carry license that, per [§400.00(2)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/400.00), permits concealed carry without regard to employment or a particular place of possession.

A carry license carries a heavier eligibility overlay. Beyond the baseline standards every applicant must meet — being of good moral character and free of disqualifying convictions — an unrestricted carry applicant faces additional recent-conduct bars. Under [§400.00(1)(n)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/400.00), a carry applicant must not have been convicted within the preceding five years of certain offenses, including specified assault, misdemeanor driving-while-intoxicated, or menacing offenses. So part of choosing "carry" is being realistic about whether your recent record clears that higher bar. Whether a specific event on your record affects a specific application is a legal question — bring it to [do I need a lawyer?](/do-i-need-a-lawyer) rather than guessing.

## The decision, worked through

Strip it to the core question: **do you need the handgun in one place, or on you in public?**

- If the answer is genuinely "in my home" or "at my business," premises is the fit. Don't apply for carry because it sounds more complete — you'd be seeking authority beyond your stated need, and the extra eligibility bars apply to you for no reason.
- If the answer is genuinely "on me, in public," carry is the license, and you should go in clear-eyed about the additional five-year conduct bars and the character standard.

Both licenses share the same underlying character requirement. Every applicant must be of good moral character — under [§400.00(1)(b)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/400.00), the essential character, temperament, and judgment necessary to be entrusted with a firearm. That standard doesn't disappear on the premises track; it applies to everyone. The carry track simply adds to it.

There's also a middle situation worth naming: people who don't live in the city but have a business here, or otherwise need to carry across city lines. That's a distinct track with its own considerations — see the [special carry license for non-NYC residents](/special-carry-license-non-nyc-residents) if that's you.

## What doesn't change either way

Whichever license you choose, most of the process is the same. You'll complete the [18-hour CCIA training](/requirements), gather notarized references and cohabitant affidavits, assemble the social-media list and safe photos, and disclose your history candidly. And no matter which license you're after, you submit your own application — a service can't file for you or represent you before the License Division, and the NYPD retains full investigative discretion over the decision.

Borough logistics are handled locally; if you're applying in Queens, our [Queens gun license guide](/gun-license/queens) covers that picture. But the premises-vs-carry choice itself is a statewide statutory distinction, identical across the five boroughs.

## The honest bottom line

Choose the license that matches what you actually need, not the one that sounds most expansive. Premises for possession in one place; carry for on-your-person in public, with a higher recent-conduct bar. If your need is narrow, a premises license is the cleaner path. If it's genuinely public carry, apply for carry and prepare for the added scrutiny. And if your own record makes the choice — or your eligibility — genuinely uncertain, that specific question belongs with a New York-licensed attorney, not a general guide.
