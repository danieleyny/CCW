Two related things trip up NYC applicants around the gun safe: the **photos your application requires**, and the **storage rules you have to follow once licensed**. For the application, [the NYPD License Division](https://www.nyc.gov/site/nypd/services/law-enforcement/pistol-license.page) makes photographs of your gun safe — door open and door closed — part of the file. Separately, [New York Penal Law §265.45](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/265.45) sets safe-storage rules you must follow after you're licensed. This guide covers both, so you handle the photos right and understand the obligation that outlasts them.

## The safe photos your application needs

The concrete, checkable requirement first: per the [NYPD License Division](https://www.nyc.gov/site/nypd/services/law-enforcement/pistol-license.page), photographs of your gun safe — one with the door open and one with the door closed — are part of the application. It's a small item that people leave to the last minute and then fumble, so it's worth doing deliberately.

The point of the two photos is to show your safe as a real, functioning place to secure a handgun. A few practical notes on getting usable images:

- **Take both shots the requirement asks for** — door closed, door open — of the actual safe you'll use.
- **Make them clear and well-lit.** A blurry, dim photo defeats the purpose. Good light, steady hands, the whole safe in frame.
- **Show the safe as it really is.** The photos are documentation, not a staged product shot.

Because you assemble and submit your own application, these are yours to take and include. A service can remind you and check that your file is complete, but no one files for you, and only a New York-licensed attorney may represent an applicant before the License Division.

If you're assembling the rest of your file at the same time — references, cohabitant affidavits, the training certificate — our [requirements overview](/requirements) shows where the safe photos sit alongside everything else, so you can knock them out with the other document items instead of as an afterthought.

## The storage rules that outlast the application

The photos are a one-time application item. The **storage obligation is ongoing** — it doesn't end when you're licensed; in a sense it begins there. Under [NY Penal Law §265.45](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/265.45), New York sets safe-storage rules you must follow once licensed.

This is an important mental shift. Many applicants treat the safe as a box to photograph for the file and then forget about. But the license comes with a continuing legal responsibility to store your handgun in accordance with New York's rules. The safe isn't a prop for the application; it's the tool you use to meet an obligation that runs for as long as you hold the license.

What that obligation requires in a specific living situation — who else is in the home, what circumstances trigger which storage rules, how the statute applies to your particular arrangement — is a legal question, and the details matter. This guide points you to the governing statute; it does not, and can't, tell you how [§265.45](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/265.45) applies to your specific household. If your situation has any complexity — for example, others living in your home, which also ties into your [cohabitant affidavits](/cohabitant-affidavit-nyc-gun-license) — that's exactly the kind of question to bring to a [New York-licensed attorney](/do-i-need-a-lawyer).

## Choosing a safe that serves both purposes

Because the safe does double duty — it's photographed for your file *and* it's how you meet your ongoing storage obligation — it's worth choosing one you'll actually rely on rather than the cheapest thing that photographs adequately. Some things to weigh:

- **A real, secure safe, not a token lockbox.** Since storage is a continuing legal duty, treat the safe as the serious tool it is.
- **Placement and access.** Think about where it lives in your home and who could reach it. Your storage obligation is about real-world security, not just having a safe somewhere.
- **The photos will show what you chose.** A sturdy, sensible safe photographs as exactly that — the two shots naturally reflect a setup you can stand behind.

Choosing well up front means the same safe cleanly satisfies the application photos and supports your storage responsibility for the life of the license — no need to upgrade later because the first one was a stopgap.

## Putting it together

Two takeaways. **For the application:** take a clear open-door and closed-door photo of your actual safe, and file them with your other document items so they're not the piece you're scrambling for at the end. **For after you're licensed:** understand that New York's storage rules under [§265.45](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PEN/265.45) are a continuing obligation, and how they apply to your specific home is a question for a licensed attorney, not a guess.

But the safe-photo requirement and the statewide storage rules are the same across the five boroughs — one a document you submit, the other a duty you carry for as long as you hold the license.
